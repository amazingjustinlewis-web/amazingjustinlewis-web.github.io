# Secret Finds: sources

Every Secret Finds card in Red Deer Rich (v0.3) tells a short, true story from Red Deer history. This file lists the
web source(s) each card's story and "read more" text were checked against. The cards themselves live in `js/board.js` (`FINDS`).

**Rules we follow:** only facts we could find in a source below; if a date or detail was unclear or the sources disagreed, it was
phrased loosely or left out (see the end of this file). Indigenous history is stated factually, from the sources, with no stereotypes.

**Eras** (design doc section 13 era skins): `pioneer` = Pioneer Red Deer, `punk2000` = 2000 Punk Red Deer, `present` = Present Day, `future` = Future Red Deer.
For now every era draws every card. Mapping used: stories up to the 1940s = `pioneer`; 1980-2005 = `punk2000`; mid-century landmarks still standing today and recent events = `present`. No history card is tagged `future` (a Future skin falls back to all cards).

## 1. Why "Red Deer"?

- **Year / era:** Long ago / `pioneer` (Pioneer Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** The Cree name for the river means "Elk River." Fur traders, likely from Scotland, seem to have mixed up the elk with the red deer back home, and the name stuck.
- **Read more (phone):** The river's Cree name is Waskasoo Seepee, and Waskasoo Creek still carries it. Red Deer is on the traditional territory of the Siksikaitsitapi (Blackfoot Confederacy), Tsuut'ina, Stoney Nakoda, Cree, Saulteaux and Métis peoples, and the area is covered by Treaty 6 (1876) and Treaty 7 (1877).
- **Effect:** Follow the creek: move to Waskasoo Park Trails.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/red-deer
  - https://www.reddeer.ca/media/reddeerca/business-in-red-deer/planning-and-development-of-property/planning/Waskasoo-Community-Plan---Final-February-10-2016.pdf

## 2. The Safe Crossing

- **Year / era:** 1884 / `pioneer` (Pioneer Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** Indigenous people knew the Red Deer Crossing as the safest place to cross the river for a long way. In 1884 Robert McClellan built a stopping house there, and in 1885 soldiers fortified it.
- **Read more (phone):** The fort takes its name from Lt. J.E. Bédard Normandeau, who commanded the 65th Mount Royal Rifles soldiers there. The North-West Mounted Police used the post until 1893.
- **Effect:** Move to Fort Normandeau.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.waskasoopark.ca/historic-fort-normandeau/about
  - https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/red-deer

## 3. Leonard Gaetz Makes a Deal

- **Year / era:** 1890 / `pioneer` (Pioneer Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** When the railway came through in 1890, Leonard Gaetz offered it a half share of the new townsite if it would build the town on his farm. It did, and the main street became Gaetz Avenue.
- **Read more (phone):** The first settlement had grown up at the river crossing. In 1891 the community moved about 7 km downstream to the new railway.
- **Effect:** Move to Gaetz Avenue.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://reddeeradvocate.com/2022/11/23/dawe-the-naming-of-gaetz-avenue/
  - https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/red-deer

## 4. The First Fair

- **Year / era:** 1892 / `pioneer` (Pioneer Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** On October 11, 1892, when Red Deer had only about 100 people, it held its first fair on Ross Street. That evening the whole community turned out for a big harvest supper.
- **Read more (phone):** Livestock was shown on the grounds beside the Wilkins Block, a two-storey building on the north side of Ross Street. The fairs grew into today's Westerner Days.
- **Effect:** Harvest supper! Collect $10 from every player.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.reddeer.ca/about-red-deer/history/history-of-red-deer/uniquely-red-deer/westerner/

## 5. 24 Wooden Pails

- **Year / era:** 1904 / `pioneer` (Pioneer Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** Red Deer's first firefighting gear was 24 wooden pails, three ladders and a hand-operated hose reel. In 1904 a fire broke out on the very night voters said no to a new fire engine.
- **Read more (phone):** The town soon formed a 16-man volunteer fire brigade. Red Deer relied on volunteer firefighters until 1969.
- **Effect:** Chip in for the fire brigade: pay $50.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.reddeer.ca/about-red-deer/history/history-of-red-deer/public-services/fire-department/

## 6. Stuck to the Seats

- **Year / era:** 1910 / `pioneer` (Pioneer Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** During Prime Minister Wilfrid Laurier's 1910 visit, a storm chased a big meeting into the Lyric Theatre. The seats had just been given fresh coats of shellac, and many people left bits of their clothes behind!
- **Read more (phone):** On August 10, 1910, Laurier drove the first spike of the Alberta Central Railway on Gaetz Avenue, until a summer thunderstorm cut the ceremony short.
- **Effect:** You're stuck to your seat. Lose a turn.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://forthjunction.ca/dawe-laurier.htm
  - https://www.reddeer.ca/about-red-deer/history/history-of-red-deer/getting-around/railroads/

## 7. A City, Quietly

- **Year / era:** 1913 / `pioneer` (Pioneer Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** Red Deer became a city on March 25, 1913, with very little fanfare. When council agreed to ask for city status, the new mayor, F.W. Galbraith, invited councillors and town staff to an oyster dinner.
- **Read more (phone):** Red Deer had about 3,000 people then, usually thought too small to be a city. When it became a town in 1901 it had just 323 residents.
- **Effect:** Your treat: pay $25 for the oysters.
- **Source(s):**
  - http://forthjunction.ca/dawe-red-deer-city.htm

## 8. Oil Fever!

- **Year / era:** 1914 / `pioneer` (Pioneer Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** When oil was struck at Turner Valley in 1914, people rushed to the Red Deer land office to file claims. The newspaper said one barber left a customer sitting in his chair! The local wells came up dry.
- **Effect:** Your well came up dry. Pay $50.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.reddeer.ca/about-red-deer/history/history-of-red-deer/businesses/oil--gas/

## 9. A Home for the Birds

- **Year / era:** 1924 / `pioneer` (Pioneer Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** Developers and timber companies wanted J.J. Gaetz's land by the lakes, but he chose to keep it for wildlife. In 1924 it became a federal migratory bird sanctuary, the oldest one in Alberta.
- **Read more (phone):** Locals call it the Gaetz Lakes Sanctuary. Its two oxbow lakes were once part of the Red Deer River, and many kinds of ducks, plus Canada geese, nest there.
- **Effect:** Move to Gaetz Lakes Sanctuary.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/migratory-bird-sanctuaries/locations/red-deer.html

## 10. Hail at the Fair

- **Year / era:** 1927 / `pioneer` (Pioneer Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** Late on the second day of the 1927 Red Deer Fair, a huge thunderstorm and hailstorm struck. South and east of the city, almost every crop was wiped out.
- **Read more (phone):** Farmers sometimes call hail "the great white combine." Alberta is "next year country," and 1928 brought one of the best harvests in years.
- **Effect:** Pay $75 for the hail damage.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://reddeeradvocate.com/2020/07/22/michael-dawe-three-minutes-of-hell-left-extensive-damage-to-region-in-1927/

## 11. A Day Too Early

- **Year / era:** 1940 / `pioneer` (Pioneer Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** In 1940 the army built a big training camp in Red Deer, a city of about 2,800 people. When it opened that October, 200 men showed up a day earlier than expected, and it was chaos!
- **Read more (phone):** After the war one of the camp's drill halls became the Memorial Centre, and the old motor pool building is still the Red Deer Armouries.
- **Effect:** Surprise guests! Pay $40 for extra supper.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://reddeeradvocate.com/2019/05/28/michael-dawe-military-camp-in-red-deer-offered-training-during-wwii/

## 12. The Green Onion

- **Year / era:** 1957 / `present` (Present Day)
- **Story (TV):** The Horton Water Spheroid, fondly called the "Green Onion," was built in 1957. It held 500,000 gallons, and at the time it was the largest spheroid-shaped water reservoir in the world.
- **Read more (phone):** It stands 40.2 metres tall and took 240 tonnes of steel to build.
- **Effect:** Move to The Spheroid.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.reddeer.ca/about-red-deer/history/heritage/community-heritage-planning/red-deers-inventory-of-heritage-sites/mountview-heritage-sites-gallery/cul---horton-water-spheroid.html

## 13. College in the Hallways

- **Year / era:** 1964 / `present` (Present Day)
- **Story (TV):** Red Deer Junior College opened in 1964 with 119 students, in borrowed space at Lindsay Thurber high school. Today it is Red Deer Polytechnic.
- **Read more (phone):** The college moved to its own campus by 1976 and took the name Red Deer Polytechnic in 2021.
- **Effect:** Scholarship! Collect $75.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://rdpolytech.ca/news/historic-60-year-timeline-rdp

## 14. A Fort for a Dollar

- **Year / era:** 1983 / `punk2000` (2000 Punk Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** In 1983 the City of Red Deer bought Fort Normandeau and nearly nine acres of land around it from the province for just $1.
- **Read more (phone):** A new interpretive centre was built beside the 1974 replica fort, and the Fort Normandeau Interpretive Centre opened in 1985.
- **Effect:** What a bargain! Collect $100.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://reddeeradvocate.com/2010/05/31/fort-normandeau-celebrates-125-years/
  - https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/red-deer

## 15. The Kid Who Loved the Woods

- **Year / era:** 1986 / `punk2000` (2000 Punk Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** Kerry Wood came to Red Deer as a boy in 1918 and grew up to write thousands of stories and newspaper columns. The nature centre that opened in 1986 is named for him.
- **Read more (phone):** For many years he was the volunteer warden of the Gaetz Lakes Sanctuary, where the Kerry Wood Nature Centre now stands.
- **Effect:** Move to Kerry Wood Nature Centre.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.waskasoopark.ca/kerry-wood-nature-centre/about
  - https://www.waskasoopark.ca/kerry-wood-nature-centre/places/interpretive-centre

## 16. Francis the Pig

- **Year / era:** 1990 / `punk2000` (2000 Punk Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** In 1990 a pig escaped a Red Deer meat plant by jumping a 1.2 m wall, then hid out along the trails for months while people across Canada followed his adventures. Today "Francis" is a bronze statue.
- **Read more (phone):** Schoolchildren wrote letters asking that he be kept safe. His bronze "ghost" was put on Gaetz Avenue in 1998 and moved near the Central Spray & Play in 2013.
- **Effect:** Escape artist! Keep this as a Tow Truck Pass. Tradeable.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.reddeer.ca/about-red-deer/history/history-of-red-deer/personalities-of-red-deer/francis-the-pig/

## 17. The Official Cookie

- **Year / era:** 1996 / `punk2000` (2000 Punk Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** Red Deer has an official cookie! In 1996 judges tasted 30 entries and picked the Caramel Surprise: a chocolate cookie with a caramel hidden inside.
- **Read more (phone):** The winning baker said biting into it is like visiting Red Deer: "You'll get a pleasant surprise."
- **Effect:** Bake sale! Collect $40.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.reddeer.ca/about-red-deer/history/history-of-red-deer/uniquely-red-deer/emblems--symbols/red-deers-official-cookie/

## 18. The Cup Comes Home

- **Year / era:** 2001 / `punk2000` (2000 Punk Red Deer)
- **Story (TV):** The Red Deer Rebels hockey team was created in 1992 to play in the new Centrium. In May 2001 they won the Memorial Cup and brought it home to Red Deer for the first time.
- **Effect:** Victory party! Collect $50.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.reddeer.ca/about-red-deer/history/history-of-red-deer/uniquely-red-deer/hockey/

## 19. Canada Winter Games

- **Year / era:** 2019 / `present` (Present Day)
- **Story (TV):** From February 15 to March 3, 2019, Red Deer hosted the Canada Winter Games, with athletes in 19 sports. It was only the third Alberta city to host them.
- **Read more (phone):** Organizers planned for more than 5,000 volunteers. Lethbridge (1975) and Grande Prairie (1995) were the first two Alberta hosts.
- **Effect:** You volunteered! Collect $20 from every player.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://www.canada.ca/en/canadian-heritage/campaigns/winter-games-2019/facts-statistics.html

## 20. The Bridge They Saved

- **Year / era:** 1908 / `present` (Present Day)
- **Story (TV):** The CPR built its steel bridge over the Red Deer River in 1908, and trains used it until 1990. Residents saved it, and after major repairs it reopened as a pedestrian bridge in September 2026.
- **Read more (phone):** City Council made it a Municipal Historic Resource in 1991.
- **Effect:** Move to CPR Bridge, 1908.
- **Source(s):**
  - https://reddeer.ca/whats-happening/news-room/historic-cpr-pedestrian-bridge-officially-reopens-to-the-community.html

## Checked but left out (uncertain or unsuitable)

- **Red Deer Rebels second Memorial Cup:** the City hockey page mentions Memorial Cup championships in both 2000-01 and 2001-02; we only use the May 2001 win, which the page states plainly.
- **City coat of arms contest year:** Dawe (forthjunction) puts the $25 contest in 1913 (winner A.B. Mitchell); the City emblems page says 1915 (Alex Mitchell). Left out.
- **Douglas Cardinal's Red Deer upbringing (St. Mary's Church, 1968):** not confirmed in a source we read. Left out.
- **Bower Ponds as old gravel pits / the 1975 Bower donation:** only seen in a search summary; the source page would not load. Left out.
- **Parking meters (about 1952):** single source. Left out.
- **North Red Deer amalgamation (1948) and Memorial Hospital (1904):** not re-checked this round. Left out of Secret Finds.
- **Michener Centre:** a painful history (eugenics, sterilization) that does not belong on a game card with a money effect. Deliberately not used.
- **Fort Normandeau:** the fort is named for Lt. J.E. Bedard Normandeau, who commanded the soldiers there (Waskasoo Park); the exact naming date is not stated, so none is given.
- **1927 hail:** "the great white combine" is a general farmers' nickname for hail in the source, not a name for that storm, so the read-more uses it only as a general nickname, never as the name of that storm.
