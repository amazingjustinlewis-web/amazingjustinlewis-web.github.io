Diary 01 · November 1, 2026

The night it worked

A space brawler that lived on the back burner for most of a lifetime, a studio that started a month ago, and the first night strangers' phones in different towns found each other and played.

Read the story →

Justin Lewis is a filmmaker and animator from Red Deer, Alberta. Over the years he has worked on projects like 9, Hoodwinked Too!, Too Human, Hot Wheels Battle Force 5, Remember (2012) and Don't Say Its Name.

For most of his life he has wanted to make games, like a fast, chaotic space brawler. It sat on the list most creative people keep: things that would be fun to do one day. The only realistic path he could picture was someone handing him ten million dollars and sixteen talented artists and checking back in a few years.

On October 2, 2026, he put a single small game online: a free web page anyone could open on a phone. That same day it got a name, Zero to Phi, and a golden-spiral logo. More games followed quickly, built with a small crew of AI companions, made for a TV in the living room and phones as controllers, with nothing to install.

Working this way felt familiar. It brought back the walks home in Toronto during 9, already dreaming about tomorrow's shot. Somewhere along the way came a quieter realisation: the big investor wasn't required anymore. The work could just start.

Home was the first test lab. Family game nights playing Red Deer Rich, a Monopoly-style board game about the city, showed what was fun and what wasn't, one round at a time.

Then came November 1, 2026. A friend near Medicine Hat opened an invite link and played Battleship. Afterwards she said it had felt like playing the computer. It wasn't. It was a person, across the province, through a web page.

Later that night three people in different places flew together in Orbit Brawl, then played Bubble Hockey with a brand-new lobby showing everyone's live ping before the puck dropped. Things went wrong too: a freeze, a zoom glitch, text too small to read and goalies too good to beat. All of it went straight onto the fix list. A friend who doesn't call herself a gamer said the one-button jumper, where each player taps their side of the screen, was fun. That may be the best review a game here can get.

No servers to rent, no store, no download. Just a link, a phone and someone on the other end. The back-burner list is starting to look like a to-do list.
