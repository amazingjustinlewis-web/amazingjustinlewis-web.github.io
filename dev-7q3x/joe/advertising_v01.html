How advertising grows with Zero to Phi

A draft for Joe, written for Justin to review first. Nothing here is final, and prices are left open on purpose.

The short version

Zero to Phi makes free games that people play together: the game is on a TV, and everyone uses their phone as the controller. Keeping the games free means the studio needs another way to pay for them. The plan is advertising that fits inside the games and the studio's Red Deer world, sold to local businesses first and growing as more people play.

It grows in three steps:

  1. Organic social announcements. The studio tells people about new games and updates on its own channels. This costs time, not money, and it builds the audience that makes ad space worth buying.
  2. Ad spaces inside upcoming games. Rink-board ads, sponsor venues that are part of a game's world, and "thanks to our sponsors" credits.
  3. Red Deer Rich board properties. Local businesses become squares on the studio's Red Deer board game, for a few years or permanently, as part of the studio's story.

Where the studio is today

  • About thirty games are in the library. One is polished and on the public site (Red Deer Rich), nine more are playable and being tested with real players, and the rest are earlier prototypes. The Game Library page shows each one with its current version.
  • The games are free to play. People scan a code on the screen and join with their phone, with no app to install.
  • Some games are already set up for cafés and bars: a TV runs the game, and guests scan a code at the table.
  • The studio has the start of anonymous play counts (how many games are played, per game and per venue, with no personal details). Once switched on, these let us give sponsors honest numbers instead of guesses.

Step 1: Organic social announcements

Each new game, update or fun moment becomes a short post or clip on the studio's own social channels. Justin handles these himself.

What it does for advertising:

  • It builds a local audience that knows the games and plays them, which is what a sponsor is really paying to reach.
  • Once there are sponsors, the posts can thank them by name, so a sponsor gets seen outside the game as well.
  • It costs nothing to run, so it can start now and keep going at every stage.

Step 2: Ad spaces in upcoming games

These are built into the games themselves rather than pop-ups or video ads that stop play.

Rink-board ads (monthly)

Hockey and other sports games have boards around the playing area, the way a real rink does. Each board is an ad space rented by the month.

  • Standard boards run along the sides of the rink.
  • Premium boards are the ones the game camera shows most: behind the goals, at centre, and in goal replays and celebrations, where the camera moves in close. Because people see these more often and for longer, they cost more.
  • The artwork can be swapped from month to month without changing the game, so a business can run a seasonal message or a special.

In-lore sponsor venues

A business can become a place inside a game's world. For example, a local bar could become a Bubble Hockey venue: players pick it from the venue list, and its name and look are on the arena where the game is played. This is closer to a sponsored location than a banner, and it suits businesses that want to be part of the community feel of the games.

"Thanks to our sponsors" credits

A calm, tidy credits panel that names the studio's sponsors. It would sit in the game menus, on the end-of-game screen and on the studio website. It's a simple entry-level option for businesses that want to support the studio without a bigger placement.

Step 3: Red Deer Rich board properties

Red Deer Rich is the studio's property-trading board game set in Red Deer, and it's the most finished game in the library. Its squares are local streets and places, and that local feel is a big part of why people enjoy it.

Local businesses could buy a square on the board:

  • For a set term, such as a few years. The business appears as a property on the board for that time, and the square can be offered again when the term ends.
  • Permanently, at a higher price. The business becomes part of the studio's lore and stays on the board in every future edition. These would be few in number, because the board has a limited number of squares.

Because the board has a limited number of squares, these placements stay rare, and the first businesses to join become part of how the game is remembered.

How it grows with the studio

  • More games mean more spaces. Each new sports game adds boards, each new world adds possible venues, and each new board game adds squares.
  • More players mean more value. As the audience grows from social posts, café screens and word of mouth, the same spaces reach more people, and prices can follow that reach.
  • Honest numbers. Anonymous play counts per game and per venue let sponsors see what they're getting, and help set fair prices.
  • Local first. Red Deer businesses are the natural first sponsors because the games are already set in Red Deer. Wider sponsors can come later if the audience grows past the city.

Suggested ground rules (for Justin to confirm)

  • Ads fit inside the game world (boards, venues, credits, properties). No pop-ups and no video ads that interrupt play.
  • Everything stays family-friendly, and the games' kid and family modes are respected.
  • Sponsors approve their artwork before it goes in, and the studio approves it too.
  • No real brand marks are used unless that business has bought the space and approved it.
  • Players' personal details are never shared with sponsors. Sponsors get anonymous play counts only.
